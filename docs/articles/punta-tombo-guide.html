Punta Tombo: Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

4 min read
Witness a Rare EventWork RemotelySee Wildlife
Home/ Argentina/ Punta Tombo: Everything You Need to Know Before You Go
On This Trip

A practical guide to visiting the largest Magellanic penguin colony in South America.

After learning about Punta Tombo, most people ask the same question:

“How do I actually get there?”

The good news is that visiting the reserve is fairly straightforward. The key is knowing how much time to allow, how the reserve is laid out, and what to expect before you set off.

Where Is Punta Tombo?

Punta Tombo lies on Argentina’s Atlantic coast in the province of Chubut, deep in the heart of Patagonia.

Most visitors travel there from one of two cities:

Trelew — about 110 km (68 miles) away.

Puerto Madryn — about 180 km (112 miles) away.

If you’re exploring Patagonia, either city makes an excellent base for a full-day trip to the reserve.

How to Get There

The easiest option is to rent a car. It gives you the freedom to arrive when the reserve opens, spend as much time as you like with the penguins, and stop at other scenic spots along the way.

If you don’t have a car, nearly every tour operator in Trelew and Puerto Madryn offers full-day excursions to Punta Tombo. These tours usually include transportation and a guide.

Public transportation is extremely limited and isn’t a practical option for most travelers, so it’s best not to rely on scheduled buses.

The final stretch of the journey includes a gravel road. It is regularly maintained and is usually suitable for standard passenger vehicles, but after heavy rain it’s worth checking current road conditions before leaving.

How Much Time Should You Allow?

A visit to Punta Tombo is best planned as a full-day trip.

Driving from Trelew takes around 1.5 hours each way, while Puerto Madryn is about 2.5 hours away.

Most visitors spend two to three hours exploring the reserve itself. If you enjoy wildlife photography or simply prefer not to rush, you’ll probably want to stay longer.

What to Expect During Your Visit

Your visit begins at the visitor center, where you can purchase tickets, learn about the penguin colony, and use the restroom facilities.

“Your visit begins at the visitor center, where you can purchase tickets, learn about the penguin colony, and use the restroom facilities.”

A large parking area is located nearby.

The main walking route is approximately 3 km (1.9 miles) long. Part of the trail follows raised wooden boardwalks, while the rest is on well-maintained paths through the colony.

Along the way you’ll see penguins nesting beneath bushes, walking between their burrows and the ocean, feeding chicks, or simply resting in the shade. Visitors must stay on the designated paths at all times, and if a penguin decides to cross in front of you, there’s only one rule:

The penguin always has the right of way.

If you’d like to understand what makes this place so special before your visit, start with our companion article, The Penguins’ Shore, which tells the story of the remarkable world you’re about to step into.

When Is the Best Time to Visit?

Punta Tombo is only open during the penguins’ breeding season.

Time of Year

What You’ll See

September

Adult penguins returning from the sea

October

Courtship, nesting, and eggs

November

Newly hatched chicks

December–January

The colony at its busiest

February–March

Young penguins preparing for their first journey into the ocean

If it’s your first visit, December and January offer the best overall experience. This is when the colony is at its liveliest, with adults, chicks, and constant activity throughout the reserve.

Good to Know Before You Go

A few practical tips can make your visit much more comfortable.

There is a small café inside the reserve, but the selection is limited, so bringing water and a few snacks is a good idea.

Restrooms are available at the visitor center.

Entry to the reserve requires a ticket. Since admission prices may change, it’s worth checking the official website before your visit.

Opening hours vary slightly depending on the season.

During weekends, holidays, and peak season, the reserve can become much busier. Arriving early in the morning usually means fewer people and better opportunities for wildlife photography.

Patagonia’s weather is famously unpredictable, even in summer. Bring a windproof jacket, sun protection, plenty of water, and comfortable closed-toe shoes.

What to See Nearby

Punta Tombo is easy to combine with several other highlights of Argentine Patagonia.

Trelew is not only the closest city but also home to one of South America’s finest paleontology museums.

Puerto Madryn is the region’s wildlife capital, offering boat trips to see whales, sea lions, dolphins, and other marine animals.

Península Valdés, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is one of the world’s premier destinations for observing marine wildlife in its natural habitat.

If you have a few extra days, these destinations complement each other perfectly and provide a deeper introduction to Patagonia’s extraordinary landscapes and wildlife.
