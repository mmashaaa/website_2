Thailand’s Pink North

4 min read
Witness a Rare EventSlow Down in Nature
Home/ Thailand/ Thailand’s Pink North
On This Trip

For a few weeks each January, wild Himalayan cherry trees turn northern Thailand's mountains pink.

Most people picture Thailand in much the same way: palm trees, tropical heat, mango groves, bustling markets and the humid air of the coast. That is precisely why it feels so surprising to discover that, just a few hours from Chiang Mai, January turns entire mountain slopes pink.

These are not Japanese cherry trees transplanted to the tropics, but wild Himalayan cherry trees, commonly known as Thai or Siamese sakura. They grow in the cool highlands of northern Thailand. For only a few weeks each year, the trees shed their leaves and cover their bare branches with pink blossoms, transforming the familiar green mountains into something altogether different.

Where to Find Thailand in Bloom

Although three destinations are usually mentioned together, each offers a very different experience. The best place to visit depends not on which name is the most famous, but on how you want to remember the journey.

Khun Chang Khian — When an Entire Mountain Village Turns Pink

For the easiest blossom trip from Chiang Mai, head to Khun Chang Khian, a small mountain village on the slopes above the city.

What makes it special is not a single grove of trees, but the way the blossoms become part of everyday village life. Pink crowns appear beside the narrow road, between houses and across the surrounding hillsides. It does not feel like a park designed for visitors. Instead, it seems as though an ordinary village has unexpectedly changed colour for a few weeks.

Khun Chang Khian is the best choice for anyone looking for a simple day trip from Chiang Mai.

Doi Ang Khang — Pink Blossoms Above Tea Plantations

Doi Ang Khang offers a very different landscape. People travel here not only for the blossom season, but for the mountains themselves.

“Doi Ang Khang offers a very different landscape. People travel here not only for the blossom season, but for the mountains themselves.”

Wild cherry trees grow beside tea plantations, orchards, misty valleys and small hillside communities. The scenery keeps changing as the road winds through the mountains: one bend reveals carefully arranged rows of tea, while the next opens onto pink trees standing against distant slopes.

Khun Chang Khian feels intimate and lived-in; Doi Ang Khang impresses through variety. It is the better choice for travellers willing to devote a full day to the journey—or stay overnight and experience the mountains at a slower pace.

Doi Inthanon — Blossoms as Part of a Larger Mountain Journey

Thailand’s highest mountain is better known for its cool forests, drifting clouds and far-reaching views. Here, the wild cherry blossom becomes part of a broader journey through the northern highlands.

It is important to know that the trees do not cover the entire national park. They bloom in particular gardens, research centres and along certain roads, so it is worth deciding on a specific destination before setting out.

In return, the day can hold far more than pink blossoms. It may also include cloud forests, mountain viewpoints and a side of Thailand that bears little resemblance to the country’s familiar beach imagery.

Doi Inthanon is the best option for those who want the blossom season to be one part of a full mountain escape rather than the sole purpose of the trip.

In brief:

Choose Khun Chang Khian for the easiest blossom trip from Chiang Mai.

Choose Doi Ang Khang for the greatest variety of mountain scenery.

Choose Doi Inthanon to combine the blossoms with a journey through Thailand’s highest mountain region.

Practical Information

January is usually the best month to visit. This is when the chances of seeing the trees in full bloom are highest.

Nature, however, does not follow a fixed calendar. The season may begin slightly earlier or later depending on the weather and elevation. Before travelling, check recent updates from the exact location you plan to visit rather than relying on photographs or reports from a previous year.

When visiting Khun Chang Khian, it is best to avoid peak weekends whenever possible. The mountain road is narrow, and heavy traffic during the height of the season can make the journey much longer and less enjoyable than expected.

Why Thai Travellers Come Here Too

January in northern Thailand is a special time not only for international visitors. Thai travellers also head into the mountains during the cool season.

Some come for the colder air, others for misty mornings and mountain views. Many come simply to witness the brief moment when familiar green slopes turn pink.

Thailand does not surprise people only with its islands and tropical landscapes. Sometimes, all it takes is climbing higher into the mountains to discover that the country has far more faces than it first appears to.
