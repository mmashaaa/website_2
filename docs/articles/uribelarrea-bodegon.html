From a Sheep Farm to a Bodegón: Things to Do in Uribelarrea

3 min read
Food Worth the TripOne-Night EscapeLike a Local

On This Trip

A working sheep farm in the morning, a long Argentine bodegón lunch by afternoon.

Buenos Aires is easy to remember for its lively streets, historic cafés, and grand avenues. But drive just an hour and a half beyond the city, and the scenery changes almost without notice. Urban neighborhoods give way to open pastures, working farms, old-fashioned bodegones, and the unmistakable aroma of food cooking over an open fire. This is how many people from Buenos Aires choose to spend their weekends.

There’s no single “right” way to experience Uribelarrea. Some visitors come to learn how cheese is made, others spend a few peaceful hours on a farm, while many arrive simply for a long lunch in a traditional bodegón. That’s exactly what makes this village special—you can create your own perfect weekend, built around whatever appeals to you most.

Visit the Farm Where Local Cheese Begins

Uribelarrea’s most memorable food experience doesn’t start in a restaurant—it starts on a sheep farm. Here, you can explore the property, see how the animals are raised, learn how the milk is produced, and follow the journey of cheese almost from the very beginning.

If your visit coincides with a cheesemaking workshop, you’ll quickly go from watching to participating. Guests learn the main stages of cheesemaking, discover why different cheeses develop distinct flavors and textures, and then taste the results of their own work. Even if you’ve never been interested in cheesemaking before, a few hours on the farm will change the way you look at an ordinary piece of cheese.

The best way to finish the visit is with a tasting. Sample several varieties, and don’t hesitate to ask the owners which cheese is their personal favorite. Recommendations like these rarely appear in guidebooks, yet they often become the most memorable flavors of the trip. If one stands out, you can buy it directly from the farm to take home.

Practical Information
📍Where to go: Weke Tambo de Ovejas
📅Before your visit: Tours and workshops run on scheduled dates. Check availability in advance.
🧀What to bring home: Farm-made cheeses are available to purchase after the tour.

Have Lunch at a Traditional Bodegón

If you’d like to understand how Argentines spend their weekends, make time for a bodegón. It’s not a fine-dining restaurant but a place where families and groups of friends gather to spend several leisurely hours eating, talking, and enjoying each other’s company.

Order an asado, homemade pasta, local sausages, or regional cheeses. Don’t be surprised by the generous portions—they’re often designed for sharing. And if a large Argentine family is celebrating at the next table, take a moment to observe. Sometimes the atmosphere tells you more about a country than any museum ever could.

Practical Information
📍Where to go: Most of the best-known bodegones are clustered around the main square.
📅Before your visit: Reservations are highly recommended on weekends.

Watch a Real Argentine Asado

Even if you’ve already tasted Argentine beef, seeing asado being prepared is an experience in itself. Massive grills, glowing coals, and rows of slowly cooking meat transform an ordinary lunch into something closer to a performance.

As the meat cooks over the fire, the aroma of wood smoke drifts through the village. Long after you’ve returned home, that scent may become the first memory of Uribelarrea that comes back to you.

Practical Information
📍Best time to visit: Weekends, when the atmosphere is at its liveliest.
🥩Don’t miss: If the kitchen is open, spend a few minutes watching the grill masters before your meal arrives.
