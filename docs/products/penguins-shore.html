Argentina · Punta Tombo, Patagonia

The Penguins’ Shore

4 min read
Witness a Rare EventSlow Down in NatureSee Wildlife
Home/ Argentina/ The Penguins’ Shore
On This Trip

On this stretch of Patagonian coast, the penguins decide who has right of way.

For most of the year, Punta Tombo looks like a place where nothing much could happen. A vast stretch of Patagonian steppe, low shrubs, relentless wind sweeping across the open land, and the Atlantic Ocean breaking against a rocky coastline. It feels too empty, too harsh, almost untouched.

Then spring arrives, and the shore changes hands.

One by one, Magellanic penguins emerge from the sea. They climb the shoreline, follow familiar paths, and return to the burrows they used the year before. Within a few weeks, what seemed like an empty coast becomes a thriving city. Punta Tombo is home to one of the world’s largest breeding colonies of Magellanic penguins. Yet its true scale isn’t something you understand from numbers. You understand it the moment you realize the penguins aren’t gathered somewhere in the distance—they’re everywhere.

A Few Months of Life

The males arrive first. They reclaim old burrows or dig new ones before the females return. Soon eggs lie hidden beneath the shrubs while the adults take turns guarding the nest and swimming out to sea in search of food. Before long, tiny chicks begin peeking out from the burrows—at first covered in soft grey down, then growing bolder and more curious with each passing week.

Summer doesn’t last long. The chicks grow quickly, replacing their fluffy down with waterproof feathers and wandering farther from their burrows every day. Eventually comes the moment when a young penguin walks to the ocean for the first time and disappears into the sea, where it will spend much of its life. As the season draws to an end, the colony slowly empties. The paths fall quiet, the calls fade away, and the shoreline once again belongs to the wind.

A City Without Buildings

The most surprising thing about Punta Tombo isn’t how many penguins live here. It’s where they choose to live.

Most people imagine penguins standing on ice or gathered along the water’s edge. Here, they leave the coastline behind and settle in the dry Patagonian steppe. Their homes are simple burrows dug into the ground beneath low bushes. Some are only a few steps from the walking paths, while others lie deeper among the scrub.

From the sea to those burrows run countless well-worn trails. Every day the penguins follow them back and forth between the ocean and their nests. Seen from above, they resemble the streets of a small town: one bird heading out to fish, another returning home to feed its chick.

Visitors don’t observe the colony from a distance.

“Visitors don’t observe the colony from a distance.”

They walk through it.

A penguin may suddenly appear from behind a bush, pause beside the boardwalk, or decide to cross the path directly in front of you. When that happens, nobody rushes it or tries to step around it. Here, one simple rule says everything about who truly owns this place:

The penguin always has the right of way.

In Punta Tombo, people aren’t the hosts. They’re simply guests passing through someone else’s town.

When Should You Visit?

Punta Tombo changes throughout the breeding season, so there isn’t a single “best” time to visit. It all depends on what you hope to experience.

Come early in the season if you want to witness the colony returning from the ocean and reclaiming the shore. A little later, the burrows are filled with eggs and busy parents. Early summer brings fluffy chicks, making the colony louder, livelier, and full of constant activity.

For a first visit, December and January are often the most rewarding months. Adults, chicks, and the endless movement between the sea and the burrows all come together to create the colony at its busiest.

By late summer, the focus shifts to the young penguins. They have grown their adult feathers and are preparing for their first journey into the ocean. It’s a quieter time—less about beginnings, more about departures.

If you’re planning your own visit, we’ve gathered everything you need to know—from the best time to go and how to get there to practical tips for the reserve—in our companion guide, Punta Tombo: Everything You Need to Know Before You Go.

When the Last Residents Leave

Every spring, Punta Tombo comes back to life.

Thousands of penguins return to a stretch of coastline that, only weeks before, seemed far too empty to hold a city. They reclaim old burrows, raise a new generation, and once again carve invisible streets between the steppe and the sea.

Then autumn arrives.

The last young penguins head toward the ocean. The adults follow. The city disappears as naturally as it appeared. The burrows stand empty, no one crosses the paths anymore, and only the wind and the waves remain.

But the silence never lasts forever.

When September returns, so do the first penguins.

They emerge from the sea, climb the shore, and the coast once again stops belonging to people.
