Build Your Own Picada in Tandil

3 min read
Food Worth the Trip
Home/ Argentina/ Build Your Own Picada in Tandil
On This Trip

Wander between delicatessens, build a picnic from cured meats and cheese, and watch the sun drop over Piedra Movediza.

Visit one or two local specialty food shops, put together your own picada, then head to Piedra Movediza to watch the sunset.

This isn’t a guided tour or a scheduled experience. All you need is a free day and the curiosity to discover Tandil from a different perspective.

What a Day Like This Looks Like

Tandil is not a place that encourages rushing. Start your day with a walk through town, stopping at a few local delicatessens along the way. One shop catches your eye with aged cheeses, another with rows of different salames. Somewhere nearby you’ll find freshly baked bread, homemade jam, or something sweet to take home.

Nobody tells you what to buy—and that’s part of the experience. Little by little, you’re not just choosing ingredients; you’re creating the mood for your day.

Once your basket is full, make your way to Piedra Movediza. Walk up the hill, find a spot overlooking the surrounding landscape, and enjoy a simple picnic. It doesn’t matter how long you stay. What matters is slowing down and settling into the relaxed rhythm that keeps drawing people back to Tandil.

Why Did Such a Strong Food Culture Develop Here?

During the second half of the nineteenth century, large numbers of Italian and Spanish immigrants settled in Tandil, bringing with them family recipes for cheeses, cured meats, and traditional charcuterie.

But heritage alone doesn’t explain Tandil’s reputation.

“But heritage alone doesn’t explain Tandil’s reputation.”

The local climate proved remarkably well suited to naturally curing salame. Long before refrigeration, the region’s cool winters, steady winds, and balanced humidity created ideal conditions for consistent production. Over time, salame making became deeply rooted in the area.

Today that connection is officially recognized. Salame de Tandil became the first meat product in Argentina to receive Denominación de Origen (DO) status—a protected designation of origin reserved for products whose qualities are inseparable from the place where they are made.

For the people of Tandil, this designation has become much more than a mark of quality. Many see it as one of the city’s defining symbols.

Create a Picada That’s Entirely Your Own

There is no perfect recipe for a picada.

Some people choose several varieties of salame. Others build theirs around a piece of aged cheese. Some can’t resist adding a loaf of fresh bread or a jar of locally made jam.

There isn’t a “correct” combination to look for. The real pleasure lies in creating one that feels right to you.

If you’re looking for inspiration, browse the Instagram accounts of local producers. They often share how they build their own picadas, which products they pair together, and how they serve them at home. Think of those ideas as inspiration rather than rules. The most memorable combinations are usually the ones you discover on your own.

Before You Go

This is a self-guided experience, not an organized tour.

There’s no need to search for the one “best” specialty shop. In fact, the fun comes from visiting several different stores and choosing whatever appeals to you.

Before setting out, simply check the opening hours of the shops you plan to visit, as well as the access hours for Piedra Movediza.
