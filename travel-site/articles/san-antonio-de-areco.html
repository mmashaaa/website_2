A Journey into Traditional Argentina: San Antonio de Areco

3 min read
Active AdventuresOne-Night EscapeLike a Local
Home/ Argentina/ A Journey into Traditional Argentina: San Antonio de Areco
On This Trip

Silverwork, an old pulpería and a gaucho museum make this the most complete gateway to criollo Argentina.

If you’d like to experience Argentina through its traditions, horses, cobbled streets, and gaucho culture, San Antonio de Areco offers enough to fill either a day trip or an entire weekend. Spend a day at a traditional estancia, discover the work of local artisans, explore Argentine art, and visit places that preserve the country’s gaucho heritage.

Things to Do in San Antonio de Areco

Spend a Day at an Estancia

An estancia is a traditional Argentine ranch, historically dedicated to raising cattle and horses. Several estancias around San Antonio de Areco welcome day visitors, offering experiences that may include horseback riding, an Argentine asado, guided walks around the property, and demonstrations by skilled horsemen. Some also offer overnight stays.

One of the best-known options is Estancia El Ombú de Areco, a historic ranch just outside town. Activities vary depending on the package you choose, so it’s important to book in advance.

Practical Information
📍Search in Google Maps: Estancia El Ombú de Areco
🚗Location: Outside the town center. Check whether transfers are available or plan to arrive by car.
⏱Time to allow: Most of the day or one overnight stay.
📅Before you go: Advance booking and confirmation of the day’s program are required.

Discover Gaucho Culture

Gauchos are the horsemen and cattle herders of Argentina’s vast pampas. Over generations, they developed their own traditions, clothing, craftsmanship, music, and horsemanship. In San Antonio de Areco, gaucho culture is far more than a museum exhibit—it lives on through local festivals, artisan workshops, estancias, and equestrian traditions.

A great place to begin is Parque Criollo y Museo Gauchesco Ricardo Güiraldes, a museum complex dedicated to gaucho life and the writer Ricardo Güiraldes. Nearby, you’ll also find Pulpería La Blanqueada, a restored country store that once served as a gathering place for locals.

Practical Information
📍Search in Google Maps: Parque Criollo y Museo Gauchesco Ricardo Güiraldes
📍Nearby: Pulpería La Blanqueada
🚶Location: Across the river from the historic center, close to Puente Viejo.
⏱Time to allow: Around 1–2 hours.
🕒Before you go: Check the museum’s current opening hours.

Visit Museo Las Lilas

Museo Las Lilas de Areco houses one of the largest collections of works by Florencio Molina Campos, whose paintings capture gaucho life, rural Argentina, and the countryside with remarkable humor and attention to detail.

Rather than presenting an idealized image of the gaucho, the museum reveals everyday life—work, horses, clothing, celebrations, and the small imperfections that make people human.

Practical Information
📍Search in Google Maps: Museo Las Lilas de Areco
📍Address: Moreno 279, Historic Center
⏱Time to allow: Around 1 hour
🕒Before you go: Check opening hours and closing days.

Stop by La Olla de Cobre

La Olla de Cobre is a family-run workshop producing handmade chocolate, caramel, and dulce de leche. Rather than being a major attraction in itself, it makes a pleasant stop between museums and a walk through the historic center.

Choose a dessert to enjoy right away or take something home. If you’re curious about the production process, ask the staff what they’re making that day and which sweet they would personally recommend trying first.

Practical Information
📍Search in Google Maps: La Olla de Cobre San Antonio de Areco
📍Address: Matheu 433, close to the town center
⏱Time to allow: 20–40 minutes
🕒Before you go: Check the shop’s current opening hours.
