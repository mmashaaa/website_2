Thailand · Srinakarin Reservoir

A Weekend That Doesn’t Need a Shore

5 min read
Extend Your WeekendOne-Night EscapeRecharge
Home/ Thailand/ A Weekend That Doesn’t Need a Shore
On This Trip

A wooden villa floating in the middle of a reservoir, reachable only by raft.

Sometimes you choose a place to stay because of what’s nearby.

It needs to be close to a waterfall, a national park, a scenic road, or a town you want to explore.

Loylum Eco Villa works differently.

Here, the villa itself is where the journey ends.

Your car stays on the shore.

You step aboard a small motorised raft, and within a few minutes the land begins to drift away.

Ahead, a wooden villa appears, floating in the middle of Srinakarin Reservoir.

That is the moment you realise the next twenty-four hours won’t resemble an ordinary countryside escape.

More Than a Floating House

The creators of Loylum set out to reinterpret Thailand’s traditional floating homes.

Their goal was to combine local craftsmanship with contemporary architecture—not simply to build another raft house, but to create a genuine private villa on the water.

The most important feature of traditional floating homes remains unchanged: complete immersion in the lake.

Inside, however, you’ll find air conditioning, a separate bedroom, a wooden soaking tub, a kitchen, a projector, and the comforts you would normally expect from a well-designed private home.

One more detail deserves attention.

The villa is powered by solar energy.

The word Eco is not just about the surrounding landscape.

Solar panels provide the electricity that allows the house to operate independently, far from the infrastructure of the shore.

You No Longer Need the Shore

The most remarkable thing about Loylum isn’t its interior but the way the entire stay is designed.

Most countryside hotels are simply a base.

You wake up, have breakfast, get into the car, and spend the day exploring elsewhere.

Here, that need almost disappears.

The lake begins the moment you step outside.

A kayak and paddleboard are already waiting beside the deck.

Your private swimming area becomes a natural extension of the terrace.

Even dinner requires no journey back to land—it is prepared by your private chef right at the villa.

The accommodation stops being somewhere you return to after your adventures.

Instead, it becomes the adventure itself, reducing the world, for a day or two, to water, timber, and the distant line of green hills.

Luxury Without Movement

Loylum is designed primarily for two guests, although a third person can usually be accommodated for an additional fee.

“Loylum is designed primarily for two guests, although a third person can usually be accommodated for an additional fee.”

The stay typically includes boat transfers, breakfast, dinner, a butler, a private chef, drinks, light snacks, a kayak, and a stand-up paddleboard.

That changes the meaning of luxury.

It isn’t only about a beautiful bathtub or a dinner prepared especially for you.

The greatest luxury is having nothing practical left to organise.

There is no need to search for restaurants, arrange a return boat, rent equipment, or worry about getting back before dark.

Everything you need already exists within this small floating world.

Every Season Feels Different

The owners do not point to a single perfect time of year.

From October to February, the weather is generally cooler and drier, making it the most comfortable season for long hours on the terrace.

From March to June, Kanchanaburi becomes much hotter.

The lake changes from beautiful scenery into the best escape from the midday heat.

Swimming, early-morning paddleboarding, and retreating indoors during the hottest hours all become part of the rhythm of the day.

From July to September, the rainy season arrives.

For many countryside destinations, that would be a disadvantage.

Here, it changes the atmosphere more than the experience itself.

The water darkens.

The distant hills sometimes disappear behind curtains of rain.

The sound of raindrops across the lake becomes part of the day.

During this season, the owners may also offer reduced seasonal rates.

Getting There

The drive from Bangkok takes around three and a half hours.

Cars remain on the shore before guests board a traditional motorised raft for the short journey across the reservoir.

The crossing takes about eight minutes.

That brief ride is what separates Loylum from an ordinary hotel.

Once you arrive, you cannot simply walk out for coffee, make a quick trip to the shop, or decide to try a different restaurant.

It is worth thinking ahead before you arrive, although most essentials are already included in your stay.

The villa offers Wi-Fi, air conditioning, a kitchen, a projector with Netflix, a private bathroom, and a wooden soaking tub.

Before booking, however, it is still wise to confirm current services, transfer arrangements, check-in times, additional guest fees, and seasonal pricing.

Who Will Enjoy It?

Loylum is ideal for people looking for an unusual overnight stay—not simply one that photographs well, but one built around a completely different way of experiencing space.

It is especially well suited to couples who want to spend one or two days without driving between attractions or following a busy itinerary.

It also appeals to travellers who value water more than constant activity.

Swimming, kayaking, paddleboarding, reading, watching films, and dining on the terrace all happen within the same small floating world.

People who quickly become restless without restaurants, cafés, or changing scenery may find the experience too isolated.

Loylum doesn’t offer an endless list of activities.

Its whole purpose is that, for a while, you no longer need one.

The Whole Journey in One Place

On most trips, accommodation becomes only a small part of the memory.

It is simply the room where you slept between drives, walks, and sightseeing.

After Loylum, that order changes.

You remember the raft carrying you away from the shore.

Then the wooden villa surrounded by water.

Dinner with nowhere else you needed to be.

A morning that began not with another journey, but already inside the most memorable part of it.

Perhaps that is why it feels inaccurate to describe Loylum as simply a floating house.

A house usually stands in one place.

Here, for a little while, it becomes the only place you need.
